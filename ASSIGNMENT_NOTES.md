# Requirement map and design notes

## C requirements

| Requirement | Where it is implemented |
| --- | --- |
| Player places towers | `DefenseGame.TryPlaceAt` and `Choose`, with `DefenseMerging.HandlePointer`: screen ray intersects the ground, then chooses a marked pad. No towers are preplaced at game start. |
| Towers fire continuously at a fixed rate | `DefenseTower.FixedUpdate`: elapsed-time cooldown from `FireRate`, whenever a live enemy is in range and can be intercepted. |
| Towers aim at an enemy | Turret gimbal rotates toward the calculated launch vector; predictive aiming upgrades direct aiming. |
| Enemies follow predetermined paths | `DefenseRoute` defines the same ordered route every game. Straight sections are joined with quadratic curves to satisfy the B-level improvement. `DefenseEnemy.FixedUpdate` follows it by distance. |
| Game ends when an enemy completes its path | `DefenseEnemy` calls `DefenseGame.EnemyEscaped` at route length; state immediately becomes `Lost`, projectiles are cleared, and the failure screen appears. |

## B requirements

| Requirement | Where it is implemented |
| --- | --- |
| Two tower types with different fire rates, projectile speeds and heights | Pulse and Siege properties in `DefenseTower`; all three required stats differ. Both use real gravity-affected projectiles. |
| Clear placement instructions | Five-step quick-start tutorial plus a persistent command deck: choose 1/2 or a tower button, then LEFT-CLICK a CYAN HEXAGON. The tutorial explains placement, cash rewards, and upgrade prices/benefits with a worked money example. H or ? HELP reopens it; the HUD always retains the selection-and-placement instructions. |
| Limited tower placement | Ten pads, ten-tower board cap, $110 starting cash, kill-only income, placement/merge/upgrade prices and occupied-pad checks. No per-wave action limit. |
| Paths are not straight | The route turns repeatedly and has sampled quadratic curves at its corners. `DefenseRoute.Position` uses cumulative distance to preserve movement speed around bends. |

## A requirements

| Requirement | Where it is implemented |
| --- | --- |
| Multiple enemy types with different speed and HP | Scout, Runner, Armored, Siege, Motorcycle and Truck statistics in `DefenseEnemy.Initialize`; distinct silhouettes/colors and health bars help identify them. |
| Non-basic assets | Saved custom polygon meshes in `Assets/Defense/Resources/DefenseMeshes`, generated by `DefenseArt` and baked by `DefenseSceneBuilder`. Drones, platforms, crystal spires, turret hulls and the curved road go beyond unmodified Unity primitives. |
| Predict target location from current position and velocity | `InterceptSolver.TrySolve`, called by `DefenseTower`: solves the time-of-flight equation with the enemy's current velocity and world gravity. |

## Predictive aiming with 3D physics

Let `r = targetPosition - muzzlePosition`, `v` be the enemy's current velocity, `g` be `Physics.gravity`, and `s` be the projectile launch speed. At flight time `t`, the required initial projectile velocity is:

```
launchVelocity = (r + v*t - 0.5*g*t*t) / t
```

The solver finds the earliest positive `t` for which:

```
|r + v*t - 0.5*g*t*t|² = s²*t²
```

It brackets the low-arc solution with short time samples, then refines it with 24 bisection steps. If no reachable solution is found, the tower waits instead of firing a zero or invalid vector. After rotating the barrel, the tower refines the solution from the muzzle's new location.

The shell then flies as a dynamic Rigidbody with gravity and continuous collision detection. It does not home, teleport, or apply damage through a distance check. Only its collision handler applies damage. Target prediction assumes the enemy maintains its current velocity during flight; sudden path turns can cause misses, which is an intentional limitation of the specified prediction model.

## Additional features

- Ten escalating waves, much larger Scout groups, fast motorcycles and heavy truck convoys.
- Kill-earned cash ($4 / $5 / $8 / $30 / $6 / $40), three tower levels with physical height growth, and 70% sell refunds. No free wave-clear bonus.
- Target priority favors the enemy farthest along the route.
- Selected-tower range indicator and hover marker for build pads.
- Enemy health bars, projectile trails, impact particles, and gold cash popups after kills.
- Title, victory, defeat, restart, pause and 2× speed controls.
- Custom neon art, readable tower cards, and a consistent interface.

These are candidates for the open-ended A+ category, not a guarantee of a grade.

## Code organization

`DefenseGame` controls state, economy, waves and player commands. `DefenseTower`, `DefenseEnemy` and `DefenseProjectile` own their respective simulation behavior. `DefenseRoute` and `InterceptSolver` isolate reusable movement and aiming mathematics. `DefenseHUD` builds the interface. `DefenseArt` uses shared materials and meshes. Editor-only build and validation scripts are under `Assets/Defense/Editor` and are excluded from player builds.

The original demo bounced random buggies around an arena and fired at clicked positions. Its scene and scripts remain available for comparison. The assignment game uses a new scene and separate scripts, replacing that demonstration loop with tower placement, route-based enemies, automatic predictive fire, campaign progression, and win/loss states.

## Harder economy and tower growth

`DefenseGame.TryPlaceAt` validates money, a free pad and board capacity. `DefenseMerging.TryMerge` checks a matching pair and cash before charging the merge fee and consuming the source. Purchases and merges can both happen repeatedly in one wave; the budget regulates growth.

`DefenseEnemy.Reward` defines the cash payout. `EnemyDestroyed` removes the enemy before awarding money, preventing double payment. `KillEarnings` supports an exact money audit. Tower upgrades spend $100 and $200 per represented tower unit, increase damage/rate/range, extend the support column and raise the actual gimbal and muzzle by 0.8 units per level. `DefenseArt.RaiseTower` adds a glowing upgrade collar. The board holds at most ten towers at once; merging and selling free pads.

## Expanded traffic and campaign

`DefenseGame.WavePlan` specifies ten waves, growing from 10 to 96 enemies. Six enemy kinds are mixed deterministically so repeated attempts are comparable. Motorcycles arrive in wave 4; 160-base-HP trucks arrive in wave 5 and become more frequent in later waves. Wave 5 is now the midpoint, and victory requires clearing wave 10. There are ten distinct pads and no per-wave purchase or merge limit.

Vehicle graphics are authored by `DefenseArt.Vehicle`. A baked 20-sided wheel mesh, rider geometry, truck cab and cargo assembly, and different lighting distinguish the two new vehicles. `DefenseVehicleWheels` animates the wheels at a rate derived from speed and radius and respects pause. Both vehicles use explicit BoxCollider dimensions to fit their bodies, while retaining kinematic path-following and real projectile collisions.

## Quick tutorial

`DefenseHUD` owns five tutorial pages, Back/Next/Skip controls and a permanent Help button. The title screen start action opens the guide, and completing or skipping it starts a clean build phase. `DefenseGame.Update` routes tutorial keys before ordinary game controls, preventing Space from both advancing a page and launching a wave. Help pauses combat and remembers whether it introduced the pause, preserving an already-paused game and the player's speed choice on exit. The persistent placement line remains visible after the tutorial closes.

## Same-type tower fusion

`DefenseMerging` owns world-pointer selection, the six-pixel drag threshold, a visual-only moving preview, valid/invalid target rings, the M/MERGE click alternative, and a single checked merge transaction. It picks towers using cached renderer bounds, so tall upgraded towers can be clicked above their pads. Drag previews contain no gameplay scripts or colliders. Cancel, invalid release, pause, tutorial, restart and game-over restore the original actor's visibility; the actual tower and firing position are never dragged around the map.

`CanMerge` requires distinct live towers, matching type and merge level, a tier below red, enough cash, and active unpaused play outside the tutorial. Paid upgrade levels may differ. Base pairs become green level 2 ($50), green pairs become purple level 3 ($100), and purple pairs become red level 4 ($200). `Absorb` doubles represented units, retains the higher paid upgrade level, includes the merge fee in investment, updates geometry and recolors the fusion rings and energy details. Red towers cannot merge further. Invalid drops spend nothing.

Damage is the paid-level damage multiplied by represented units. Fusion also compounds shot rate by 20% per merge tier, 5% compounded range per merge tier, and 18% width/depth per tier. Fusion adds no height; only paid upgrades raise the column and muzzle. Paid upgrades remain capped at level 3 and can still be purchased for a merged tower below that cap. Merge strength comes from consuming existing purchased towers, and charging merge fees.

## Animated example and tougher traffic

The last tutorial page embeds `DefenseMergeDemo`, an isolated visual scene rendered into a UI RawImage. It uses the actual tower geometry and a looping cursor/drag/widening sequence, labels the merge levels and example cash, and has a Replay button. The preview uses a dedicated camera/layer, runs on unscaled time so paused Help still animates, and contains no DefenseTower actors or economy calls. It cannot modify the player's towers or cash.

Traffic now totals 522 enemies, with counts and spawn rates listed in `WAVE_GUIDE.md`. The complete automated run makes eight purchases and two actual merges, ending with six actors representing eight purchased units. Its money audit includes all consumed tower investment and confirms no injected campaign funds.

## Opening-wave balance regression

The original expanded opening relied too much on the precise placement and upgrade timing used by the automated campaign. Dedicated tests reproduced two reasonable Pulse openings failing without upgrades. Wave 1 now uses ten 3-HP Scouts at speed 2 with 1.4 seconds between spawns; normal Scout stats resume in wave 2, and all later wave compositions remain unchanged. `DefenseStarterHint` highlights the left interior pad with a gold ring and a readable billboard. It is a suggestion, not a placement restriction.

`DefenseOpeningValidation` buys exactly one unupgraded tower, makes no further purchases and injects no money or damage. It tests Pulse and Siege on the entrance-side, left-interior and central pads. All six clear the training wave, including with graphics enabled. The full campaign regression is also rerun separately.

## Audio and light Scouts

`DefenseAudio` is initialized once in `DefenseGame.Start`. It maintains one independent looping background source and 32 reusable shot sources. Original Pulse/Siege laser WAVs live in `Resources/DefenseAudio`; `DefenseTower.FixedUpdate` calls `PlayShot` for every launched shell. Pitch rises two semitones per paid upgrade and three per merge tier; each voice has independent pitch and stereo position. Music remains at pitch 1 and its source survives game resets. The supplied Kingdom Hearts Dearly Beloved recording is imported as streaming audio at `Resources/DefenseAudio/DearlyBeloved`, starts at the title screen and loops continuously.

`DefenseArt.LightScout` replaces the Scout's old tracked bug-like model with an enclosed light vehicle, cyan circuitry, four glowing wheel discs and a short light trail. It reuses authored wheel meshes and `DefenseVehicleWheels`. Gameplay statistics and the original collider are preserved.
